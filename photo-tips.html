How to photograph your rental

You do not need a camera. A phone, daylight and a few habits are enough to show your place clearly. Here is what we would do.

Shoot in daylight, with the lights on and the blinds open

Natural light is the best light for a phone camera. Open every blind and curtain, then switch on the lamps so shadowed corners do not go dark. Avoid the flash, which flattens a room.

Choose the time of day for the outside

Photograph the exterior with the sun behind you or to one side, so the front of the building is lit, not in shadow. Early morning and late afternoon are softer than midday. If you can, pick a day when the ground is dry.

Hold the phone level

A tilted phone makes walls lean and doorframes look crooked. Turn on the grid in your camera settings and line it up with a door edge or a corner. Keep the phone upright, not tipped up or down.

Stand in a corner, at chest height

From a corner you can show the whole room and how it fits together. Hold the phone at about chest height. Shooting from near the ceiling or the floor changes how the room feels.

Skip the extreme ultra-wide lens

The widest setting stretches the edges and makes small rooms look larger than they are. Guests notice when they arrive. The standard lens, or a slight step wider, shows the room honestly.

Clear away what is only temporary

Before you shoot, move cables, toiletries, bins, laundry and remote controls out of the frame. Smooth the bed and straighten the cushions. These are not part of the place, and removing them lets the room speak for itself.

One photo per room, in a logical order

Show each room once, from its best angle, and avoid near copies. Lead with your strongest room, often the living space or the best view. Then follow the order a guest would walk through the home.

Clean the lens

A phone lives in pockets and bags, and the lens collects smudges. Wipe it with a soft cloth before you start. A smudged lens makes photographs look hazy, and it is the easiest fix on this list.

Show what guests actually get

Photograph the place as it is, including the view, the bathroom and the size of the bedrooms. If something is worn or unusual, do not hide it. Guests who find what the photographs promised leave better reviews.
