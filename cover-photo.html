How to choose your cover photo

The cover is the first photo a guest sees, and often the only one they judge you by at first. Here is how we would choose it.

  • Pick the photo that shows what makes your place different
  • Make sure it is bright and sharp at a small size
  • Frame it horizontally, with room to be cropped
  • Skip close-ups of decor and photos with people
  • Keep it honest
  • Look at your options small, next to other listings
  • Refresh it when the season changes

Pick the photo that shows what makes your place different

Ask what a guest cannot get anywhere else nearby. It might be the view, the fireplace, the hot tub, or the room where guests will spend most of their time. Lead with that, not with the hallway or the front door.

Make sure it is bright and sharp at a small size

Most people first see your cover as a small thumbnail. Dark corners and soft focus turn to mush at that size. Choose a photo with good daylight and a clear subject you can still make out when it is tiny.

Frame it horizontally, with room to be cropped

Covers are usually shown in a wide frame, and the edges can be trimmed. Keep the important part toward the middle so the photo still reads if it is cropped. A tall photo rarely works as a cover.

Skip close-ups of decor and photos with people

A close-up of a pillow or a lamp does not tell a guest what the place is like. Photos with people, even family or friends, draw the eye to them and away from the home. Show the space itself.

Keep it honest

The cover is a promise about what guests will find. Choose a photo that matches the place as it is today, with the same view, the same furniture and the same light you can offer. Guests who find what the photo showed are happy to be there.

Look at your options small, next to other listings

Try two or three covers. View each one small on your phone, next to listings in your area, the way a guest would. Pick the one that is clearest at a glance and still looks like your place.

Refresh it when the season changes

If the outside is the main draw, such as a lake, a garden or a snowy porch, a cover from the wrong season can look out of date. Swap in a current photo when the view changes, as long as it still shows the place honestly.
